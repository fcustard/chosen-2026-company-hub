Approximate Running Time: 4½–5 minutes Musical Sequence: “Brotherhood” — approximately 1½–2 minutes Characters: JOSEPH, LEVI, MALACHI, CALEB, EZRA, MARY, SHIRA

TRANSITION FROM SCENE 4

One beat.

BANG!

BANG!

A second strike.

BANG-BANG!

Lights cut to Joseph's work area. The tonal shift should be immediate: warmth, laughter, physical energy.

JOSEPH,

LEVI, MALACHI, CALEB, and EZRA are already working together. The unfinished table from Scene 2 is now unmistakably the project.

JOSEPH'S WORLD.

Bright.

JOSEPH,

pieces are lifted;

someone climbs;

someone ducks;

WORK BEFORE WORDS

Do not begin with dialogue. Let the audience watch the men work.

Joseph checks a length. Levi takes weight. Ezra checks alignment. Malachi steadies a piece. Caleb tries to help and is just useful enough to remain employed.

The physical vocabulary beneath the sequence is MEASURE -> MARK -> CUT -> SET. It may be expressed through movement rather than repeated as lyrics.

Hammer strikes, scraping wood, footwork, hand-claps, and safe prop percussion begin to align into a musical pulse.

“BROTHERHOOD” BEGINS

The carpentry pulse now expands into the full Brotherhood sequence.

This should feel rehearsed and exciting, but never disconnected from the work. The men are not presenting choreography to the audience; they are working so instinctively together that the work becomes choreography.

Movement possibilities: passing wood in counter-rhythm; coordinated lifts; safe tool-handling phrases; turns generated by carrying material; foot-stomps; hand-claps; bench/wood percussion; a repeated lift-set-release pattern; Caleb adding unnecessary flourishes that the others repeatedly correct.

Joseph should eventually stop supervising every beat and fully join the physical joy. We need to see him young, competitive, laughing and completely at home with these men.

CHOREOGRAPHIC FEATURE

The music expands into approximately 20–30 seconds of instrumental choreography.

This is the major visual payoff of the scene.

MOVEMENT LANGUAGE

Use:

sharp foot patterns;

synchronized stomps;

body percussion;

tool percussion;

bench work;

rhythmic passing of lumber;

jumps over low work pieces where safe;

sliding underneath beams;

controlled vaults by trained performers;

quick level changes;

canon movement;

tight unison sections;

athletic turns;

five-man formations that constantly break and reform.

The choreography should feel as though construction itself has become dance.

One man throws a lightweight piece of wood.

Another catches it on count.

Hammer.

Spin.

Pass.

Stomp.

Clap.

TRANSITION INTO SCENE 6

Mary and Joseph are alone.

No blackout.

No ominous music.
