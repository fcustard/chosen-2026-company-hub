NOTES

Scene 6 needs to be one of the most carefully acted scenes in the entire production. The source architecture is very clear that Joseph must begin in concern, not accusation; he actively searches for another explanation because he wants Mary’s truth to fit the woman he knows. Only after exhausting those possibilities does the pain become personal.

The other key rule I’m preserving is that the song does not solve the argument. “It’s Not Mine” lets Joseph express what he cannot contain, but the actual separation happens afterward in quiet dialogue.

The key dramatic line I added is

JOSEPH: “I thought I knew what ‘whatever comes’ meant.”

That gives the Scene 2 promise its first painful payoff without destroying it permanently. In Scene 2, “Whatever comes, I'll walk with you” sounds romantic because neither of them has imagined a cost this great. In Scene 6, Joseph discovers that he made a promise before he understood what it might require. Then Scene 8 can finally let him make that promise again with full knowledge of the cost. That gives the motif a real three-scene dramatic arc.

I also kept Joseph’s moral decision exactly where the source architecture calls for it: even believing Mary has betrayed him, he refuses to publicly humiliate her. His righteousness is demonstrated before he receives divine confirmation, which makes his later return much more meaningful.

And visually, the Scene 6 progression should be very controlled: they begin relatively close, move farther apart as trust deteriorates, occasionally attempt to cross the gap during the song, and finish completely separated. This is an acting scene first; the physical distance itself becomes part of the storytelling.
