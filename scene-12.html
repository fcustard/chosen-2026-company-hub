The revised shepherd dialogue should now be much easier to rehearse. The Young Shepherd's essential lines are intentionally short: “A little,” “I know,” “I'm cold,” “Good,” “What's that?”, “A manger?”, “Let's go,” “A manger,” “There!”, “In a manger,” “You know?”, “The manger,” “We found Him,” “What's His name?”, and “Jesus.” His acting—not memorizing paragraphs—can carry the wonder of the scene.

NOTES

In this scene, it starts small and human, expands through the shepherds, lands Joseph’s public naming of Jesus, then deliberately shifts from historical realism into theatrical epilogue with Nia and Simon, the Wise Men, the returning company, and the “Welcome to Nazareth” finale. The source specifically supports beginning with stillness, treating the shepherds as real working people, and allowing the finale to expand gradually rather than overwhelming the manger from the beginning.

One important update from the older source draft: Joseph does not name Jesus before the shepherds arrive. We are protecting the newer locked public naming payoff: a shepherd asks the child’s name, and Joseph publicly answers, “Jesus.”

The source strongly supports protecting the manger as the final emotional center instead of letting spectacle overtake it. It specifically recommends a layered final picture with Mary, Joseph and Jesus central, the surrounding company at multiple levels, the star above, and a deliberate hold before blackout.

The strongest new dramatic payoff in this version is the name sequence

YOUNG SHEPHERD

What is His name?

Joseph looks at the child. Then Mary. Then the shepherds.

JOSEPH

Jesus.

MARY

Jesus.

That is stronger than naming Him immediately after birth because the name now becomes the moment the story leaves the private world of Mary and Joseph and enters the world. The shepherds repeat it, then carry it outward.

And I would definitely keep both Nia and Simon in the epilogue. Scene 1 began with two children welcoming us into an ordinary town. Scene 12 ends with those same two children realizing that the story was far bigger than anyone in Nazareth understood. That gives the entire musical a clean theatrical frame.

This guide helps directors and volunteers quickly understand each character and which performer may fit the role best.

How to Use This Guide

● Start with the person, not the look. We want performers who make the characters feel real.

● For the main roles, acting and connection with the other actors matter just as much as singing.

● A strong singer is not automatically the best choice for a role if the acting does not feel believable.

● Smaller roles can be played by the same actor when it does not confuse the audience.

● The 14 main recurring characters should remain distinct so the audience can recognize them throughout the story.

WHAT MATTERS MOST WHEN CASTING: Tells the story well → Feels real → Connects with other actors → Can sing the part → Can handle the movement → Looks the part

Main and Featured Characters

MARY

Who this person is: Mary is the heart of the story. We watch her go from an ordinary young woman to someone facing an unbelievable calling, fear, gossip, heartbreak, faith, reconciliation, and motherhood.

Who to look for: Look for a young woman who feels honest and natural onstage. She needs to be emotionally open, a strong actress, and a strong storyteller when she sings. She does not need the biggest voice in the room, but she must be able to carry several important songs and the emotional weight of the show.

Important: She must have believable chemistry with Joseph. Do not cast Mary based only on voice or appearance.

JOSEPH

Who this person is: Joseph loves Mary, but then has his world turned upside down. He has to deal with hurt, confusion, faith, forgiveness, and eventually becoming Mary's protector and partner.

Who to look for: Look for a young man who feels grounded, kind, believable, and emotionally strong. He needs to show pain without simply becoming angry. He should sing well and work naturally with Mary and his group of friends.

Important: Chemistry with Mary is essential. Joseph needs vulnerability as well as strength.

GABRIEL

Who this person is: Gabriel is the messenger who tells Mary that God has chosen her. This moment changes the entire story.

Who to look for: Look for someone with a calm, confident presence and a clear speaking voice. Gabriel should feel caring and trustworthy, not cold or overly dramatic.

Important: Avoid the stereotypical booming 'angel voice.' Gabriel should feel connected to Mary.

ELIZABETH

Who this person is: Elizabeth is Mary's safe place. She is older and wiser, and she supports Mary when Mary feels frightened and alone.

Who to look for: Look for a mature woman who naturally feels warm, steady, trustworthy, and spiritually grounded. She should be a very good listener and make Mary feel safe.

Important: She should not sound like she is preaching every line. Her strength comes from warmth and understanding.

NIA - CHILD STORYTELLER

Who this person is: Nia helps welcome the audience into Nazareth and guides them through parts of the story.

Who to look for: Look for a confident child, roughly ages 8-12, who speaks clearly, connects easily with an audience, has good timing, and can sing and move comfortably.

Important: Do not choose Nia just because a child is cute. This child needs real stage confidence and storytelling ability.

SIMON - CHILD STORYTELLER

Who this person is: Simon works with Nia to help tell the story and introduce the audience to the world of Nazareth.

Who to look for: Look for a confident child who works well with Nia but has a different personality. Simon can be more grounded, curious, or matter-of-fact, so the two children balance each other.

Important: Cast Nia and Simon as a team. They should share attention instead of competing for it.

SHIRA

Who this person is: Shira is the caring friend. She notices what Mary is feeling and stands by her even when she does not fully understand what is happening.

Who to look for: Look for someone warm, sincere, loyal, and emotionally grounded. She should feel like the friend Mary would trust when life falls apart.

Important: Do not make her weak or overly sweet. Shira has quiet strength.

TALIA

Who this person is: Talia is the thinker. She asks the questions everyone else may be afraid to ask because she is trying to understand Mary's unbelievable story.

Who to look for: Look for someone smart, direct, quick, and believable. She needs to challenge Mary without seeming cruel.

Important: Talia is not the 'mean friend.' Her questions come from concern.

ADINA

Who this person is: Adina is the practical friend. When something happens, she immediately starts thinking about what needs to be done next.

Who to look for: Look for someone clear, organized, grounded, and able to move a scene forward naturally.

Important: She should feel helpful, not bossy.

DAFNA

Who this person is: Dafna is the most expressive friend and brings some natural humor to the group. Her reactions help release tension.

Who to look for: Look for someone with natural comic timing, warmth, and expressive reactions who can also become serious when the situation calls for it.

Important: Do not choose someone who tries too hard to get laughs. The humor should feel real.

LEVI

Who this person is: Levi wants to protect Joseph. His first instinct is to keep Joseph from being embarrassed or hurt.

Who to look for: Look for someone direct, loyal, confident, and strong who has good chemistry with the other men.

Important: He is not a bully. Even his hard advice should come from caring about Joseph.

MALACHI

Who this person is: Malachi is the spiritually grounded friend. He is the one who tells Joseph not to make a major decision before asking God.

Who to look for: Look for someone calm, thoughtful, sincere, and believable when talking about faith.

Important: He should sound like a concerned friend, not a preacher.

CALEB

Who this person is: Caleb reacts from the heart. He is warm and emotional, and he can be naturally funny because he often says or shows what everyone else is feeling.

Who to look for: Look for someone with good reactions, warmth, humor, and strong group chemistry.

Important: He needs to be able to move from funny to serious without feeling fake.

EZRA

Who this person is: Ezra is the logical one. He wants the facts to make sense, even when the situation does not make sense at all.

Who to look for: Look for someone clear, precise, thoughtful, and naturally dry or funny.

Important: Do not make him cold. Logic is simply the way Ezra tries to help.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

Who this person is: The Roman Announcer brings the census order and helps push Mary and Joseph toward Bethlehem. The role can also bring humor.

Who to look for: Look for someone with a strong voice, confidence, good timing, and the ability to control a crowd.

Important: The humor works best when the Announcer takes the job very seriously.

ANGEL VOICE / ANGELIC MESSENGER

Who this person is: This voice delivers God's message during Joseph's dream and may also be used in later heavenly moments.

Who to look for: Look for someone with a clear, calm, memorable voice. Strong speaking and singing ability is helpful depending on how the final music is written.

Important: Keep the delivery simple and certain rather than overly dramatic.

INNKEEPERS 1-3

Who this person is: These are the people who turn Mary and Joseph away in Bethlehem. Together, they build pressure and make the search for a place to stay feel harder and harder.

Who to look for: Look for three strong character performers who can each make their innkeeper feel different, even with limited stage time.

Important: Do not make all three innkeepers the same joke or personality.

INNKEEPER 4 / FINAL LODGING KEEPER

Who this person is: This is the final person Mary and Joseph meet. Although there is no normal room available, this person helps them find shelter.

Who to look for: Look for someone warm, grounded, and compassionate who can make a small role feel important.

Important: The audience should feel the change from rejection to kindness.

INNKEEPER 4'S WIFE / BETHLEHEM WOMAN

Who this person is: She helps Mary as the birth approaches and adds warmth and human care to the Bethlehem scenes.

Who to look for: Look for a mature woman with a calm, caring presence and good nonverbal acting.

Important: This is one combined role unless the script later gives us a clear reason to split it into two people.

SHEPHERD 1

Who this person is: A grounded shepherd who helps lead the group after the angel appears.

Who to look for: Look for a sincere actor with natural leadership, warmth, and a little humor.

Important: The shepherd should feel like a real working person, not a Bible-story stereotype.

SHEPHERD 2

Who this person is: A shepherd who begins to wonder whether the angel's message might really be true.

Who to look for: Look for a warm, curious performer who listens well and works well with the group.

Important: It is helpful if this performer feels different from Shepherd 1.

SHEPHERD 3

Who this person is: The more skeptical or practical shepherd. This character helps make the supernatural moment feel human.

Who to look for: Look for someone with grounded humor, strong reactions, and the ability to move from doubt to wonder.

Important: Do not keep playing the comedy once the scene becomes meaningful.

YOUNG SHEPHERD

Who this person is: A younger shepherd who adds a child or teen point of view to the angelic announcement.

Who to look for: Look for a confident child or young teen who listens well, speaks clearly, and can stay focused in a large scene.

Important: This role is recommended in the current character plan.

WISE MEN 1-3

Who this person is: The Wise Men appear in the finale as an important visual part of the Christmas story.

Who to look for: Look for three performers with strong stage presence who can communicate through posture, movement, and facial expression.

Important: They do not have speaking lines in the current plan.

Community and Ensemble Roles

Gossipers / Featured Villagers: People in Nazareth who react to what is happening and help show how quickly news and rumors travel. Look for actors with clear voices, good reactions, and personality.

Villager Man / Villager Woman: Featured townspeople who help make Nazareth feel like a real community. Look for dependable actors who can handle a few lines and react naturally.

Village Elder: A respected person in the community. Look for someone with natural authority and a calm presence.

Parents / Mothers: Adults who help create the family life of Nazareth and interact naturally with the children.

Featured Children: Children who may have a few lines. Look for clear speech, confidence, focus, and the ability to follow direction.

Children's Ensemble: The current plan calls for about 8-10 children. They should be dependable, energetic, comfortable singing and moving, and able to stay focused.

Marketplace Workers / Merchants: Performers who help make Nazareth feel busy and alive. They should be comfortable acting while doing simple tasks, moving, and reacting.

Travelers / Journey Ensemble: Performers who help show the trip to Bethlehem. They need to communicate tiredness, crowds, families traveling, and the difficulty of the journey through acting and movement.

Bethlehem Travelers / Families / Lodging Guests: The crowd that makes Bethlehem feel full and helps us understand why Mary and Joseph cannot find a room.

Additional Shepherds: Extra performers who can make the shepherd scene feel larger while allowing Shepherds 1-3 to remain the featured characters.

Dancers: Strong movers who support the big musical moments and transitions. We want dancers who can follow choreography, stay together, and help tell the story.

Praise Team / Choir / Heavenly Voices: Strong singers who give the finale a bigger, uplifting sound. Blending together and following musical direction are more important than trying to stand out.

Full Company: Everyone in the production. Even when someone is not featured, they should stay focused and help make the world of the show feel real.

Simple Doubling Plan

Some smaller parts can be played by the same performer. This helps us use the cast well without confusing the audience. For example, some Scene 9 villagers can later become Scene 10 travelers, Scene 11 innkeepers, or Scene 12 shepherds/company members. The 14 recurring characters, Mary, Joseph, Gabriel, Elizabeth, Nia, Simon, Shira, Talia, Adina, Dafna, Levi, Malachi, Caleb, and Ezra, should remain separate performers because the audience follows their relationships throughout the story.
