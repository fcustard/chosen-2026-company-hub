PREPARE FOR YOUR PART

Choose the path you need today.

You may use more than one path. Your published call and rehearsal leader’s instructions guide what to prepare.

I’m actingScenes, spoken cues, and blocking
  1. Open My scenes. Read the full scene, your cues, and the stage directions. Switch to All scenes when you need the wider story.
  2. Practice spoken cues: read the cue, say your line aloud, then reveal it. Mark difficult cues Need work and use that view for another pass.
  3. Review any rehearsal videos your leader assigns. Bring your script or notes, a pencil, and questions about blocking.

Blocking means your planned movement onstage. Song lyrics remain in the full scripts.

I’m singingLyrics, rehearsal tracks, and entrances
  1. Read the full scene and its lyrics so you know what happens around your song.
  2. Open My tracks and use the published version your music leader assigns. Vocal and instrumental versions are labeled; switch to All tracks when needed.
  3. Practice your assigned lyrics and entrances. Bring uncertain words, harmonies, and cues to music leadership.

For spoken dialogue, use Lines & Cue Practice. For lyrics, use the scripts and published music.

I’m dancingAssignments, choreography, and scene context
  1. Check your dance assignment and read the scene. Being in the dance ensemble does not mean every dance call includes you.
  2. Review the assigned dancer rehearsal videos for choreography, counts, and spacing. Use the published track your leader assigns.
  3. Bring appropriate rehearsal clothes, safe shoes, water, and anything your call requests. Ask dance leadership about uncertain steps or formations.

If a video or track is missing, ask your dance leader what to practice.

I’m on the production teamDepartment work, appointments, and backstage support
  1. Find your department and confirm your assignment with its lead. If your name is missing from the Home selector, ask your lead to confirm your call.
  2. Review upcoming production work. Planning windows and milestone targets do not release a personal fitting appointment or confirm completed work.
  3. Confirm when and where you are needed, what to bring, and who receives your update. Follow your department lead and Stage Management.

Read the costume, prop, and backstage procedures. Use technical equipment only when trained and assigned.

I’m a parent or guardianChild’s call, preparation, check-in, and pickup
  1. Choose your child’s name on Home. On a shared device, check the selected name each time and switch it for another family member.
  2. Check the child’s personal report and scheduled-through times. If pickup timing is missing or unclear, confirm with production; don’t use the overall event window as their release time.
  3. A parent or guardian signs the minor in and out and remains reachable. Review the children and youth procedures, and help your child open the preparation path for their part.

ID may be requested. Minors stay with designated leaders and follow the established restroom procedure.

QUICK ANSWERS

When something is unclear.

My name, role, or material looks wrong.

Check the selected name on Home. Then review your role, current scripts, and published tracks. Ask your rehearsal or department leader, or use Production Help. Don’t guess from an older version.

I’ll be absent or late. What should I do?

Use the Report Absence / Late form as soon as you know. Identify the call you will miss, then review any material assigned while you were away.

Are my checklist and practice marks sent to production?

No. Preparation checks are your reminders, saved on this device for each person and rehearsal. Cue-practice marks are saved for each spoken role. They don’t confirm attendance or replace your leader’s instructions. When browser storage is unavailable, the page explains that marks last only for that visit.

Can I rely on a screenshot or subscribed calendar?

Check My Calls again before you leave. Old screenshots, messages, printouts, and calendar subscriptions can lag behind changes. Use published personal times; ask production about a pending call or missing time.

What do cue, blocking, off book, and places mean?
Cue
The line or action that tells you when to begin.
Blocking
Your planned movement onstage.
Off book
Performing without reading the script.
Places
Go to your assigned starting position and be ready.
Who should I tell about an injury or safety concern?

Tell the Stage Manager or a Director immediately, then complete the Incident Report as directed. Don’t wait for an online form response to raise an urgent concern.
