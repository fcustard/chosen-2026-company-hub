The team may still be deciding who needs to attend. A confirmed rehearsal does not mean everyone is called. A missing call does not mean you are excused. Check the full schedule and ask production. Don’t guess your arrival or pickup time.

PREPARE FOR YOUR PART

Choose the path you need today.

Choose the section that fits your role. You can use more than one. Follow your call and your leader’s instructions.

I’m actingScenes, lines, and stage movement
  1. Open My scenes. Read the whole scene and the notes about movement. Choose All scenes to see the rest of the story.
  2. Practice your lines. A cue is the line or action that tells you to begin. Read the cue, say your line, then reveal it to check. Mark hard lines Need work and practice them again.
  3. Review any rehearsal videos your leader assigns. Bring your script or notes, a pencil, and questions about blocking.

Blocking means where and when you move onstage. Find song lyrics in the full scripts.

I’m singingLyrics, rehearsal tracks, and entrances
  1. Read the full scene and its lyrics so you know what happens around your song.
  2. Open My tracks. Use the version your music leader assigns. Vocal and instrumental versions are labeled. Choose All tracks to see the full list.
  3. Practice your words and when to start singing. Ask your music leader about any words, parts, or cues you don’t understand.

For spoken dialogue, use Lines & Cue Practice. For lyrics, use the scripts and published music.

I’m dancingAssignments, choreography, and scene context
  1. Check your dance assignment and read the scene. Being a dancer does not mean you attend every dance rehearsal.
  2. Review the assigned dancer rehearsal videos for choreography, counts, and spacing. Use the published track your leader assigns.
  3. Bring appropriate rehearsal clothes, safe shoes, water, and anything your call requests. Ask dance leadership about uncertain steps or formations.

If a video or track is missing, ask your dance leader what to practice.

I’m on the production teamDepartment work, appointments, and backstage support
  1. Find your team and check your job with its leader. If your name is not on Home, ask your leader when to come.
  2. Check the next production tasks. A team work window or target date is not your personal call or fitting appointment. Old plans do not prove the work is finished.
  3. Ask when and where to come, what to bring, and who needs your update. Follow your team leader and the Stage Manager.

Read the costume, prop, and backstage procedures. Use technical equipment only when trained and assigned.

I’m a parent or guardianChild’s call, preparation, check-in, and pickup
  1. Choose your child’s name on Home. On a shared device, check the selected name each time and switch it for another family member.
  2. Check your child’s own call. “Report time” means arrival. “Scheduled through” means the planned end. Ask production if pickup time is unclear. The full rehearsal may last longer than your child’s call.
  3. A parent or guardian must sign the child in and out and stay reachable by phone. Read the children and youth procedures. Help your child find what to practice.

You may be asked for ID. Children stay with their assigned leaders and follow the restroom rules.

QUICK ANSWERS

When something is unclear.

My name, role, or material looks wrong.

Check the selected name on Home. Then review your role, current scripts, and published tracks. Ask your rehearsal or department leader, or use Production Help. Don’t guess from an older version.

I’ll be absent or late. What should I do?

Use the Report Absence / Late form as soon as you know. Identify the call you will miss, then review any material assigned while you were away.

Does production see my checklist or practice marks?

No. They are saved only on this device. Preparation checks are separate for each person and rehearsal. Line-practice marks are saved for each spoken role. They do not tell production you will attend. Follow your leader’s instructions. If your browser cannot save marks, the page tells you they last only for that visit.

Can I rely on a screenshot or subscribed calendar?

Check My Calls again before you leave. Old screenshots, messages, printouts, and calendar subscriptions can lag behind changes. Use published personal times; ask production about a pending call or missing time.

What do call, cue, blocking, off book, and places mean?
Call
Your instructions for when and where you are needed.
Cue
The line or action that tells you when to begin.
Blocking
Where and when you move onstage.
Off book
Performing without reading the script.
Places
Go to your assigned starting position and be ready.
Who should I tell about an injury or safety concern?

Tell the Stage Manager or a Director immediately, then complete the Incident Report as directed. Don’t wait for an online form response to raise an urgent concern.

Your part is an act of service.

Onstage, backstage, or helping a child, use your gifts to serve others and honor God. Read 1 Peter 4:10–11.
