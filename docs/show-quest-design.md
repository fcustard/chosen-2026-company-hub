# Show Quest: design and rollout

## Review of Character Quest

The first version made at-home acting practice easy to find. It worked for silent actors and kept checks on the player's device. Its stars came from tapping a task, though. The player had no surprise, choice inside a task, or reason to change a second try. It did not serve singers, dancers, or production crew. The rehearsal check named only acting skills and did not offer a next move.

## Design choices

| Source | What it suggests | Show Quest choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ryan and Deci, [Self-Determination Theory](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/the-theory/) | Support choice, growing skill, and connection. | Pick a path and mission; see personal progress; help the next person. |
| Scott Nicholson, [A RECIPE for Meaningful Gamification](https://scottnicholson.com/pubs/recipepreprint.pdf) | Play, choice, reflection, and connection can matter more than rewards alone. | Try a mission twice, meet a twist, choose a next move. |
| Yu-kai Chou, [Octalysis](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-examples/octalysis-gamification-framework/) | Meaning, creativity, and feedback can drive play; loss pressure is a different driver. | Show how a part helps the show; use no streak or leaderboard. |
| Khan Academy, [Mastery System](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631-What-are-Course-and-Unit-Mastery) | Make skill progress visible over repeated work. | Three mission gems per path; replay changes the twist; coach checks show dated practice. Gems mean played, not mastered. |
| Education Endowment Foundation, [Feedback](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/16-19/developing-independent-learners/feedback) | Feedback should point to a specific next action. | Coach marks one observed skill and one thing to try next. |
| Duolingo, [Friends Quests](https://blog.duolingo.com/friends-quests/) | Shared goals can connect people with different learning work. | Pass It On asks every player to help the next person's part. There is no public team tally. |

These are design influences, not a claim that any one method has been tested in this production. The acting videos supplied by the directors informed the Act path. We adapted their ideas for stage and beginner safety. We did not include emotional recall, pressured breathing, or camera-only advice.

## The game in one minute

1. Choose Act, Sing, Dance, or Make the Show. Players with several jobs can switch paths.
2. Choose Cue Hunt, Make It Clear, or Pass It On. Play in any order.
3. Try Round 1. Reveal a small twist. Try Round 2.
4. Choose one next move. A gem shows that both rounds were tried. Replay for another twist.
5. At rehearsal, show a coach one skill. The coach marks **Still working** or **Clear today**, then picks a next move. The actor sees dated checks on that device.

No task asks for a video upload, cast ranking, daily streak, or private story. Crew prompts say to stop and ask a lead when a cue or safety step is unclear. The current script, track, and leader's notes remain the source for show details.

## Example journey

An ensemble dancer opens Show Quest and picks **Dance → Cue Hunt**. At home they count the approved entrance and show the first move. They reveal the twist: the music is quiet, so they watch the count. They try again, choose **Notice more**, and earn a gem for completing both rounds. At rehearsal, a coach watches that entrance, marks **Still working**, and chooses **Notice the cue**. On the next rehearsal date, they can mark **Clear today**. Both checks remain visible in order.

## Privacy and release checks

The Hub is public and static. Show Quest stores progress in this browser's local storage, separated by the person selected in Company. Guest progress stays under Guest on that device. Earlier Character Quest checks remain in their original local storage and appear in a read-only section. Clearing Show Quest does not erase those earlier checks. A coach cannot remotely see another phone, and the feature is not a private director dashboard. A shared view would need an authenticated service and a separate review of youth privacy and consent.

Before wide rollout, test the game on a phone and desktop with one person from each path. Check the no-lines route, guest mode, switching paths, replay, save/revisit, and the 30-second coach check. Watch one real rehearsal to see if the game prompts help or add work; adjust the wording if needed.

## Short rollout message

> We made a small game for the whole CHOSEN team. Open **Practice → Show Quest** on the Hub. Pick your path and play one five-minute mission at home. At rehearsal, show your next move to a coach for a quick check. You can act, sing, dance, or help make the show. No lines are needed. Gems mean you tried, and you can play again. Your progress stays on your device.
