# CHOSEN: five-person website review

**Status: NOT RUN — no participant feedback has been collected.**

The short written welcome is live. Automated checks and internal-browser checks are separate from this human review. Do not mark this review complete until five different people have tried the site and their observations have been recorded.

## Who should take part

Use five adult volunteers: one actor, one singer, one dancer, one production team member, and one parent or guardian. Include at least two people who are new to the Hub. Where possible, include someone who is less confident using a phone. One person trying five roles is not a five-person review.

Allow 10–15 minutes per person. Use their usual phone or computer. This plan does not authorize contacting or scheduling anyone; participants still need to be arranged.

## Opening words for the reviewer

“We’re checking how clear the website is. We are testing the site, not you. Please say what you are looking for as you go. If something is hard to find or understand, that helps us improve it.”

Start at [the Company Hub](https://fcustard.github.io/chosen-2026-company-hub/index.html). Do not explain which buttons to press. Stop before any form is submitted. Avoid playing audio aloud without checking with the participant.

## Tasks

1. **Understand the welcome.** Ask the person to open Start Here and read the welcome. Then ask: “In your own words, why are we making CHOSEN? What is the story meant to help people understand?” Do not suggest an answer first. Check whether they identify Jesus, the Savior, and the invitation to trust Him.
2. **Find their call.** Ask: “Show me how you would find when and where you are needed next.” Have them choose their own name, or a suitable example agreed with the facilitator. If a personal time is missing, ask what they would do. A correct response is to check or ask, not invent an arrival or pickup time.
3. **Explain the wording.** Ask what “report time” and “scheduled through” mean. Ask whether a confirmed rehearsal means everyone must attend. Record the words that confused them.
4. **Find material for their role.** Actor: open an assigned scene and find spoken-cue practice. Singer: find an assigned track and its lyrics. Dancer: find their dance assignment and rehearsal resources. Production team member: find their department and explain the difference between a planning window and a personal appointment. Parent/guardian: find the child’s call and the check-in/out guidance.
5. **Use a reminder.** Ask the person to check and uncheck a preparation item. Ask where that check is saved and whether it tells production they will attend. For an actor, also try marking a cue Need work and finding that practice view. Do not overwrite existing progress; use a fresh browser profile when needed.
6. **Find help.** Ask where they would go if their name or material were wrong, or if they would be absent. Have them stop before submitting a form.

For a parent, also ask them to show how they would switch to another family member. For a person whose name is missing, count finding the correct help route as success; do not change the roster to complete the test.

## Questions at the end

- What was hardest to find or understand?
- Which words would you change?
- How easy was this to use, from 1 (very hard) to 5 (very easy)? Why?
- What would you check before leaving for rehearsal?

## When this review is complete

Completion means five distinct people have finished their sessions and real observations have been recorded. It does not mean the site automatically passed.

Fix anything that could cause a wrong arrival, pickup, call, or fitting assumption first. Next, fix repeated confusion about the message, navigation, or role materials. Ask affected participants to retry the changed task. Mark each issue as fixed and rechecked, or still open. Keep the final summary honest about anything unresolved.
