CONTINUOUS FROM SCENE 8

No traditional blackout.

Mary and Joseph are walking home together.

The familiar sound of Nazareth begins before the marketplace is fully visible.

HAMMER. BASKET. FABRIC SNAP. CLAP.

The same work vocabulary from Scene 1.

The same stalls. The same baskets. The same fabric. The same people.

For a breath, it feels like home.

Mary and Joseph enter at the edge of the marketplace, still holding hands.

One villager notices.

Then another.

A conversation slows.

A woman stops folding fabric.

A merchant looks up.

No one has become a villain. They simply know—or think they know.

Mary feels the change in the room before anyone says anything.

Her hand tightens around Joseph’s.

Joseph answers with his own.

They keep walking.

“WHAT WILL HE BE?” BEGINS

“WHAT WILL HE BE?” — MUSICAL TERRITORY

The children lead. Warm, playful, imaginative. The number is entirely about possibility.

The adults remain in the marketplace, some working, some watching, some whispering.

The song must not explain who Jesus becomes. The audience supplies that knowledge.

A Villager Man steps forward. Not a villain. A neighbor who believes he is saying the obvious thing.

THE SAME TOWN — DIVIDED

The ensemble reorganizes without becoming a cartoon of good people and bad people.

Near Mary and Joseph: Shira, Adina, Dafna, Talia, Caleb, Malachi, and Ezra.

At a distance: skeptical villagers, whisperers, people who simply do not know what to think.

Levi remains between the two groups.

Nobody wins the argument.

Nazareth does not apologize.

The gossip rhythm begins to peak.

A Roman drum cuts through the marketplace.

Everything stops.

BOOM.

A trumpet or unmistakable imperial sound.

Rome does not care about Nazareth’s scandal.

ROME ENTERS

The Roman Announcer enters as though his arrival is unquestionably the most important event of the day.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

People of Nazareth!

A few villagers are still murmuring.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

People.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

Of.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

Nazareth.

The village quiets.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

Thank you.

He unrolls the decree.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

By decree of Caesar Augustus—

A villager groans.

The Announcer stops and looks directly at them.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

Your enthusiasm is noted.

CALEB

We haven’t even heard it yet.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

And still you’ve disappointed Rome.

A few villagers react.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

A census shall be taken. Every household is to be registered according to family line and ancestral city.

Immediate murmuring.

TALIA

We have to travel?

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

If your ancestral city is elsewhere, yes.

LEVI

Who pays for the journey?

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

Excellent question.

Beat.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

You do.

The village groans.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

You will present yourself, your household, and the required family information in the appropriate city.

VILLAGER

When?

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

Immediately.

CALEB

Immediately?

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

It is a wonderful word.

Beat.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

It means now.

The marketplace explodes into practical anxiety: counting, packing, questions, arguments.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

Govern yourselves accordingly.

He begins to roll the decree.

ROMAN ANNOUNCER

Thank yooou.

He exits as the Roman drum resumes.

BETHLEHEM

The marketplace is now moving for an entirely different reason.

Mary looks at Joseph.

He is doing the calculation before she asks.

MARY

Joseph?

Beat.

JOSEPH

Bethlehem.

Mary knows what that means: distance, travel, timing.

MARY

When do we leave?

Joseph looks at her.

His first instinct is to protect her.

JOSEPH

Mary—

MARY

When?

He sees that she is not asking whether she is going.

She has already decided that.

Joseph exhales.

JOSEPH

As soon as we can.

Mary nods.

Their hands find each other again.

Around them, Nazareth prepares to move.

END SCENE 9

NOTES
